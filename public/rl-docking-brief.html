TECHNICAL METHODOLOGY & ANALYSIS

Closing Loopholes the Learner Found

LOOPHOLE 01 · SKEWED DOCKING

Two-Point Fork Axis Measurement

The agent learned it could get its fork tips within 2 cm of the target without squaring up, because the original reward didn't constrain heading relative to the slot. We added the slot heading error directly to its 16 scalar inputs and measured error across two distinct points along the fork axis, strictly enforcing a square, parallel entry.

LOOPHOLE 02 · WALL PIVOTING

Contact Sensors & Proximity Penalties

In ultra-narrow 2.0 m corridors, the robot discovered it could physically bounce against the wall to whip its rear chassis around. We added contact force sensors, terminated the episode immediately on any hard impact (9.4% crash rate), and applied a shaping penalty for getting dangerously close to walls.

LOOPHOLE 03 · HOVER EXPLOITATION

Hold-the-Dock Time Incentive

Because ending the episode on successful dock terminated reward collection, the learner learned to hover indefinitely right next to the slot without docking. We restructured the reward to pay the agent for every continuous second it holds the docked position, driving median docking completion time down to 5.7 seconds.

PROBLEM SPECIFICATION & TIGHT-SPACE CURRICULUM

Docking a 1.65 m Pallet Truck in a 2.0 m Corridor

ROBOT MORPHOLOGY & TOLERANCE

The robot is a tricycle pallet truck with one steered drive wheel at the chassis end and two fork tines on load wheels at the other end, spanning 1.65 m in overall length. It docks fork-first, reversing into a slot. To count as docked, it must stop with both tines within 2 cm of the slot centreline and square to it.

STEP-BY-STEP CURRICULUM TIGHTENING

The robot drives along a corridor and reverses 90° into a slot on either side. We tightened the space step by step: the corridor from 2.5 m to 2.0 m wide, then the slots from 2.2 m to 1.6 m. For a 1.65 m truck, a 2.0 m corridor leaves negligible room for the swing, requiring near-zero-radius pivoting maneuvers.
